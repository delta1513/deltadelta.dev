I want to preface this with stating that the journey of improving your mental health is endless, and the characterisation I use here is to signify a distinct change in thoughts, feelings and behaviours based on my own observations.

It's been about 4 months now, maybe longer ever since I started my journey to "fix" my mental health. It started after my experience in Canada where I regretted not engaging with a number of experiences. This led me to start doing something about it, and let's just say the first attempt wasn't very well thought-out if you take a look at my [now page](https://deltadelta.dev/writing/now/) a little after that time.

Looking back on that chaos, it really seemed that I was imprisoned by my own mind. I was heavily disconnected with myself and the world around me. As a result, I couldn't find the words to describe the feelings, and since I was living on my own the whole time, there was no way to soothe the pain. Substance abuse was on my mind for the longest time, however, through sheer willpower I stayed away from it. At the time, I couldn't put into words why, but there was a bulb in the back of my head that kept telling me it was a means to an end. So I stayed away from it, and unintentionally faced my thoughts head-on.

Therapy came next, and probably the most I have to say about it is that it's not for me, but acted as a platform I could build up from. After the first two sessions, I figured out how they go about it, what strategies they use to help, so I just did that with myself. That way it didn't cost me so much money and I could have a much faster feedback loop to get progress quicker.

Then came the ultimate period of rumination. I feel like I rapidly drifted into seclusion. As if I rejected the world and went into my own dark cave in the side of the mountain. This was a good thing in my eyes. If I were to describe it like my work, it would be as if there is an open incident that is directly affecting customers. What do you do? - You put down everything, all distractions, all other priorities, and put all resources towards understanding the problem (not fixing it). So that's exactly what I did, and it just came naturally.

All that was an exaggeration, and I think you might know by now that I like to be a little poetic sometimes. In reality, it looked like this:
- Reducing social interactions (especially with family)
- Reducing time spent on distractions
- Reducing overall distractions by changing my environment
- Excessive journaling
- Trying new experiences like meditation
- Obsessively limiting other sources of soothing mental pain

My thoughts at the time were solely focused on the surface level, looking at what was actually happening and my front-line thoughts. Things like:
- I'm pathetic
- I don't deserve what I have
- The world around me is uninteresting
- I can't complain because I have everything

I think there's a book called "I have no mouth but I must scream". I think I should read that someday...

So like an onion, one-by-one, I peeled back the layers. It almost felt that every week, I made a new realisation, like I figured out a deeper meaning of why I had that original thought. This was honestly exhilarating. It was using my favourite skill (intellect) to give meaning to things. It connected me to myself more and more, after my whole life of rejecting it.

Next came countless pages in my diary and at least a weeks worth of watch time on self-help YouTube videos. I was searching for something. I believe I was searching for meaning, something that explains why this was all happening. Also known as understanding. This understanding is synonymous with connectedness. It shows that you are congruent with your own thoughts and the reality around you. So every week, I would slowly become more and more congruent.

Then at some point in the recent weeks as of writing this, my psyche made a profound revelation. This will be depicted in detail in one of my meditations, so I'll keep it short here, but I founded my own theory of connectedness. Understanding what that word actually means and the power it has to shape your life. Once this realisation was made, no longer did I feel the desire to watch self-help videos, I didn't need to self-soothe as much, and I didn't need to keep looking for more and more ways to "fix" myself. My ultimate realisation was that I wasn't broken in the first place, rather I was simply disconnected.

So from here, my journey took me on the path to find connectedness.

Now I have to make evident here, I've been deliberately vague with what this connection actually refers to. To me, it refers to the physical and metaphysical understanding of the world around you. So not just being "connected" with your friends by sending them "how are you?" every now and then to let them know you're alive, it's understanding what causes them grief, finding shared pain. To extend this beyond social interactions, I am personally very proud of myself for being so interested in science and technology very early on in my life, as I believe it to be one of the most divine connections. Being able to look at reality around you and watch it follow the rules and equations that you learned yourself, then staring in marvel at the beauty of something you can't explain just yet.

No longer was I living in a world at face-value. No longer was I trying to conform. No longer did I perceive myself as a glitch in the system.

I am now in search of something deeper, a comfortable understanding of everything around me.

---

I often like to think of life now like a journey through uncharted forests in an endless mountainous terrain, and I hope that I can get you to see this as vividly as I do in my own mind.

From a bird's-eye-view, you watch as the rivers form valleys and lead to lakes, the mountains have trees up to a point until they become barren and topped with grey-blue rock and snow. The sky is crisp, few clouds and a cleanness unlike anything else. The air is cool, not too warm and not too cold.

When you look off into the distance, you will see a horizon lined with a cool haze of mist, like an ethereal bathing ground. You know that no matter how far you travel, you will probably see the same thing. But maybe there's an ocean out there? A desert perhaps?

Wildlife is flourishing, but mostly beneath the trees. You see a few swallows floating about in the sky, moving somewhere off into the distance, but that's about it. It's quite peaceful and delightful when you see it from this height, and especially during this time and weather.

But you aren't here. You are on the ground. You have your backpack, a few supplies, some food and tools. It's just you, and you are the last one alive. Sometimes it's rainy or stormy, it gets cold at night, and you are always on-edge that you will find something that might actually be a match for your strength.

Here on the forest floor, you are shaded by trees and shrubbery. It's fairly easy to walk around, there aren't many places you can't go, but because you still need to survive, you frequent a few (like rivers and lakes). You can walk around and you will see new things, new animals, flowers, plants, a historical ledger of the survival of everything around you.

What will you do here?

You could stay in one spot, build a treehouse and a little farm to keep yourself comfortable. Befriend the wildlife perhaps, and occasion trips to the nearest summit so that you can observe the beauty around you.

But could you see and examine every bird? Could you smell every flower? In time for your departure from this world of course. This is merely impossible as we know it today.

So if you were to make it your goal to get as far in one direction as possible, for example, you would smell some flowers, but let others wilt. Traverse some paths, but never tread a single toe on others. Scale some inclines, but let others scream into a cold and empty void alone. It's simply too much to bear onto your primitive brain when you hold the endless fear of constantly walking through this forsaken land, only to find the same endless forest.

This is truly a profound experience, and we all live it subconsciously every day. Please think about it.
